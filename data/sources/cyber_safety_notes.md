
# ScamShield — Cyber Safety Knowledge Base

## Source S001: Digital Arrest Scam Awareness

Source: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Home Affairs
URL: https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2020570

Key information:
- Scammers impersonate law-enforcement or government officials.
- They may use video calls, threatening messages, and fake official settings.
- Victims may be pressured into following instructions or transferring money.
- Suspicious incidents should be reported using official cybercrime channels.

## Source S002: Protecting Personal Information

Source: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Home Affairs
URL: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2280197

Key information:
- Do not share bank information, passwords, OTPs, or credentials with unknown or unverified callers.
- Fraudsters may impersonate law-enforcement agencies.
- Verify suspicious claims through independently obtained official contact details.

## Source S003: Cybercrime Reporting

Source: National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal
URL: https://cybercrime.gov.in/

Key information:
- Cybercrime complaints can be submitted through the government portal.
- The national helpline 1930 supports reporting of cyber financial fraud.
- Official reporting information must be checked before publication.

## Fictional Example: Fake Internship Offer

A fictional student receives a message offering an internship.
The sender demands an upfront registration fee and pressures
the student to pay immediately.

This is a synthetic awareness scenario, not a real incident
or a verified case study.

Red flags to illustrate:
- Unexpected upfront payment request
- Pressure to act immediately
- Unverified recruiter identity

The scenario is illustrative; a fee request alone is not
conclusive proof that an offer is fraudulent.


## Source S004: Online Job Scam Warning Signs

Source: Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)
URL: https://cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/theme/resources/advisories/ADVISORY%20TAU-ADV-004_Captcha%20Filling%20Fraud_08.04.2025.pdf

Key information:
- Fraudulent online job schemes may promise high earnings for minimal effort.
- Some fraudsters demand registration, training, or software fees upfront.
- Warning signs include upfront payments, unrealistic earning promises, and delayed or denied payouts.
- People should investigate the legitimacy of unfamiliar job platforms and avoid sharing sensitive personal or financial information.

Scope:
This advisory specifically discusses fake CAPTCHA-filling jobs.
The points are useful as examples of online recruitment fraud,
but do not establish that every internship requiring a fee is fraudulent.
